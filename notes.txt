For spectral power distribution, and other measurements of light, they often put wavelength on the
x-axis, but use a normalized y-axis of unspecified unit. Or maybe in radiant flux (W).
It is still a bit confusing because ther are many terms that are named similarly.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_energy#SI_radiometry_units
Generally, you want to use terms from photometry and colorimetry.

Terms that start with "luminous" come from photometry, while terms that start with "radiant" come from radiometry.

The rendering equation is defined in terms of radiance.
This is nice, because their units appear more intuitive.

The procedure for conversion from spectral radiance to luminance is standardized by CIE and ISO.

Candela (cd) is the unit of luminous intensity.
It is luminous power per unit solid angle.
A common wax candle has a luminous intensity of roughly 1 cd.

Lumen (lm) is the unit of lumionous flux or luminous power.
lm = cd . sr
